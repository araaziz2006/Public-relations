About Our Department

History & Overview

The Department of Public Relations was established in 2021 to produce professional academic cadres and fulfill market requirements. Both theoretical and practical aspects are highly emphasized. Given the critical role of public relations in both private and governmental sectors, specialized modern techniques are applied. The department focuses on key market-driven subjects such as political and strategic consultancy, institutional management, innovation, brand management, consumer behavior research, reputation protection, campaign management, and crisis communication.

Creator's Note

Why Did I Create This Website?

When I finished 12th grade, I searched extensively to find a department that matched my passions and background. Among several choices, Public Relations caught my eye—but since it was a brand-new department at the University of Sulaimani, finding clear information online about the syllabus, courses, and careers was extremely difficult.

After joining the university and experiencing the professional curriculum, inspiring professors, and especially our head of department who possesses profound expertise, I realized I made the absolute right choice. Therefore, I built this platform so future students won't face the same lack of information and can make informed choices.

— Ara Aziz

Practical Learning

🏆 Featured Campaign: "Share a Coke"

How PR strategies drive global success for top multinational corporations.

Coca-Cola's Historic Campaign

In 2011, Coca-Cola faced declining youth sales. The PR team replaced their iconic logo on bottles with 250 popular first names, transforming mass-market products into personalized items.

💡 The PR Secret:

Instead of conventional advertising, they fostered emotional connection. Over 500,000 user-generated photos flooded social media, boosting sales by 2.5% after years of decline.

Takeaway: True PR shapes consumer behavior organically without feeling like direct advertising.

Quick Answers

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Real answers to common questions asked by prospective students.

1. What is the language of study?
Most core modules are taught in Kurdish, alongside specialized English terminology and courses to strengthen students' linguistic competence.
2. Can both Literary and Scientific high school graduates apply?
Yes, graduates from both literary and scientific tracks are eligible to apply through Zankoline or Parallel systems.
3. What is the difference between PR and Journalism?
Journalism focuses primarily on reporting and broadcasting news, whereas Public Relations centers on reputation management, strategic communications, crisis response, and stakeholder relations.
